## Story
Someone has stolen the world's color. A bunny knight explores it with the **Chromoscope**, a viewer with two colored discs that tints whatever she looks at and reveals what the theft left hidden.

## Protagonist
- **Who she is:** a bunny knight. Name: _(pending)_.
- **What sets her apart:** she has no powers of her own; every ability comes from the filter she is wearing (see [[Mechanics]]).

## Target audience
- **Age:** 10 and up.
- **Habits:** short sessions and browser games.
- **Interests:** puzzles, platformers and accessible games.

## USP
- **Mix, don't just switch:** two discs give 6 filters, each with its own ability.
- **Hear the color:** each filter adds chords to the music.
- **Color is also danger:** an enemy of your filter's color vanishes from sight, but it is still there.
- **A literal *Shift*:** you shift the color by turning the discs, and the perspective changes with the second disc.
- **Accessible by design:** playable with one hand, fully remappable, and works with a gamepad and on touch screens.

## Similar games
| Game | Similarity | Difference |
|---|---|---|
| *Hue* (2016) | Changing the color makes objects of that color appear or disappear | In *Hue* you pick one color from a wheel; here two discs are mixed, each color grants an ability and changes the music |
| *Fez* (2012) | Changing perspective is the core mechanic of its 2D puzzles | *Fez* rotates 3D space; here you "rotate" the color, and the second disc changes the view |
| *Celeste* (2018) | 2D platformer in short screens, with strong focus on accessibility | *Celeste* is a precision platformer; here the challenge is the color puzzle |
| *Hollow Knight* (2017) | Visual reference: atmosphere and a 2D knight | It is a combat metroidvania; ColorShift has no combat |
